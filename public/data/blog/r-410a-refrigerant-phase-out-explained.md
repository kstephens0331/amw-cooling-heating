# The R-410A Refrigerant Phase-Down: What Texas Homeowners With Older AC Systems Need to Know

*Published July 21, 2026, by AMW Cooling & Heating LLC*

---

If you have researched a new AC system recently, or a technician has mentioned refrigerant type during a repair visit, you may have run into unfamiliar terms like R-32 or R-454B alongside the R-410A your current system likely uses. The refrigerant used in residential air conditioning is changing industry-wide. Here is what is actually happening, what it means for a system you already own, and whether you need to do anything about it now.

## What R-410A Is

R-410A is the hydrofluorocarbon refrigerant that has been the residential air conditioning standard since the early 2010s, when it replaced the older R-22 refrigerant. If your AC system was installed any time from roughly the early 2010s through today, it almost certainly runs on R-410A. It has been a reliable, well-understood refrigerant for over a decade, and the overwhelming majority of residential systems currently in service in Texas use it.

## Why the Refrigerant Is Changing Again

R-410A is being phased down for new equipment under the federal AIM Act, which directs a nationwide reduction in the production and consumption of high global-warming-potential (GWP) hydrofluorocarbons. R-410A has a relatively high GWP compared to newer alternatives, which is the reason manufacturers are moving away from it for new equipment.

This is fundamentally a manufacturing and regulatory shift, not a recall or a safety issue with equipment already installed in homes. It is similar in spirit to the earlier transition away from R-22, which also played out over years rather than overnight.

## What R-32 and R-454B Are

R-32 and R-454B are the lower-GWP refrigerants that manufacturers have adopted for new 2025-and-later residential AC and heat pump equipment. Both are part of the industry's response to the AIM Act's phase-down targets. As new equipment comes to market, it increasingly ships with one of these refrigerants instead of R-410A.

For homeowners, the practical difference shows up mainly at the point of new installation. If you replace your system going forward, the new equipment you are quoted will very likely use R-32 or R-454B rather than R-410A, simply because that is what manufacturers are now producing.

## Does This Mean You Need to Do Anything With Your Current System?

If your existing AC uses R-410A and is running normally, there is nothing you need to do right now. This transition affects new equipment manufacturing, not the legality or safety of operating equipment you already own. Systems already installed can continue to be serviced, maintained, and repaired by a licensed contractor as they always have been.

A few things are worth keeping in mind as the transition continues:

- **Routine maintenance and repairs on an R-410A system are not affected.** Refrigerant leak detection, repair, and recharge on existing equipment continues as normal, performed by an EPA 608-certified technician.
- **When you eventually replace your system, expect the quote to reflect newer refrigerant equipment.** This is not something you need to request. It is simply what manufacturers are shipping.
- **A refrigerant change is not, by itself, a reason to replace a working system early.** The decision to replace should be driven by your system's age, repair history, and efficiency, the same factors that mattered before this transition began.
- **Ask any contractor which refrigerant your quoted equipment uses.** It is a fair question, and a straight answer is a good sign you are dealing with a contractor who keeps up with the industry.

## Why This Matters for Installation, Not Just Equipment

Refrigerant type affects more than the label on the unit. Proper handling requires EPA 608 certification, and a correctly charged system, whatever refrigerant it uses, depends on accurate commissioning after installation. ## The Bottom Line

If your AC currently runs on R-410A and is working well, you do not need to take any action because of this transition. It is a manufacturing-side change that will show up naturally the next time you need a new system, not something that affects the equipment already cooling your home today.
